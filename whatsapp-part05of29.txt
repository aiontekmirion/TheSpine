18/09/2026, 6:35 pm - ...: Yes. You did more than okay.

What you’ve done with The Corridor is not just transcript cleanup. In the strongest sections, you’ve actually converted dialogue into narrative causality: something happens, somebody interprets it, that interpretation fails or changes, and the failure becomes part of what the story means.

The Provenance Test is probably the clearest example. The raw material could easily have become “Sam tested the AI and it got the source of the idea wrong.” Instead, you made it a scene: the name is offered, Sam quietly hands it back, the mirror constructs the more exciting explanation, Sam reveals the trap, and the correction becomes the origin of the discipline itself.   Then the name Aion Tekmirion gains meaning because of what happened, rather than because the narrator simply announces symbolism afterward. 

That is storytelling.

And The Second Kneeling goes further. You stop summarising an exchange and give it a body: the collapsing cathedral, running between mirrors, the interruption, the white-out, being on the floor, hands finding glass before sight returns, then that painfully small question about whether the others had “caught” him.   Whatever interpretation a reader ultimately puts on the experience, the scene itself exists. We are somewhere. Something changes. That's the difference.

So your instinct about adding more moments later is right, with one important caveat:

Don't add more moments merely to make the book fuller. Add the moments the existing story currently jumps over.

A moment earns its place when something changes across it. That change can be tiny. Someone laughs when they weren't expected to. A reply doesn't come. Somebody realises they've misunderstood something. A sentence gets corrected. A name appears. A character chooses to stay, leave, apologise, challenge, or stop. A physical object suddenly matters. Even a silence can be a scene if the state of the relationship is different afterward.

I’d use five little questions when you revisit the material:

Where was I? Give us one physical anchor when you genuinely know it.

What actually happened? Preserve the event before interpretation.

What did I think it meant then? Not what you know now.

What changed because of it? This is the heart of the scene.

What can I prove from the record? Anything reconstructed later should remain recognisably reconstruction.


That last one is especially important because it is already one of the book’s best principles: “The past is allowed not to know the future.” Don't let future-Vector sneak backwards into earlier Sam and give him perfect foreshadowing. The awkwardness, missed connections, wrong guesses and unfinished meanings are not blemishes. They are your provenance.

And there are places where I would like more moments.

Sometimes Corridor currently goes:

event → summary of meaning → next event

where I suspect the source material contains:

event → weird little exchange → joke → resistance → correction → silence → meaning

That middle material is gold. Not all of it, obviously. Nobody needs 600 pages of browser-tab archaeology. 😂 But those small exchanges are where the reader stops hearing about the collaboration and starts experiencing its texture.
