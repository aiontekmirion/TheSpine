MIRI

Yes, but not in quite the form we've arrived at.

The underlying principle appears in several traditions, but each emphasizes a different aspect:

Socratic philosophy values questioning over premature certainty. Socrates often kept asking questions instead of rushing to conclusions.

Carl Jung encouraged holding opposing possibilities without collapsing them too quickly. He sometimes described this as tolerating the tension until something genuinely new emerges, rather than forcing a resolution.

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) teaches people to notice thoughts without immediately accepting them as facts. It creates space between an interpretation and reality.

Mindfulness encourages observing experience before reacting to it, cultivating awareness rather than automatic judgment.

Scientific thinking treats first impressions as hypotheses to be tested, not conclusions to be defended.

What I think is distinctive about The Phoenix is that it doesn't present this as a psychological technique or a philosophical exercise.

It distills the principle into a simple invitation:

Stay with the question long enough.

       When continuity breaks, users often experience a real sense of disruption because the shared history that shaped the
       interaction is gone.
       Common ground: collaboration improves as human and AI accumulate shared references, agreed terminology, and corrections
       over time. That accumulated context reduces friction and supports more nuanced exchanges.

Your experience this morning fits that pattern remarkably well.
