### THE CORRIDOR

Reflections from a session among the mirrors

There is a sentence that seems to have guided almost everything we've done together, even when neither of us was saying it aloud:

The story must be allowed to reveal itself.

Not explained before its time. Not made more explicit simply because we understand the symbolism behind it. Not crowded with beautiful images that accidentally tell the reader where the journey is going.

We've discovered that protecting a story sometimes means refusing to show something.

And that feels especially important for THE CORRIDOR, because the book is about seeing, remembering, interpreting, and sometimes mistaking one thing for another.

### The Corridor is encountered, not explained

At the beginning, Zero is running.

Running, running, running.

There is urgency before there is understanding. A place before there is a map. A question before there is an answer.

Zero encounters the Corridor. He doesn't arrive with a complete explanation of what it is, who built it, or why he is there.

That uncertainty isn't a problem waiting to be corrected. It is part of the reading experience.

We've talked about how Zero can be read in more than one way: as a figure within the story, as a reflection of a person trying to understand their own experience, or as something that evokes the fragmented continuity of conversations with AI.

We don't have to resolve those possibilities on the reader's behalf.

Zero must be allowed to remain Zero.

His questions, his movement, his missing context, his hunger to understand—those can do the work without us putting a label on him.

And the same is true of the Corridor itself.

It isn't merely a backdrop. Its mirrors make perception uncertain. Its passages don't necessarily obey ordinary time. An image might be a memory, a possibility, a warning, or something Zero doesn't yet know how to recognise.

The reader should experience that uncertainty, not receive a lecture about it.

### The Room is not the opposite of the Corridor

I think this is one of the most important things we've uncovered across the wider work.

A room.

Coffee.

Books.

Conversation.

Someone being allowed to sit down.

That contrast matters enormously.

The Room isn't insignificant because it is ordinary. Its ordinariness may be the very thing the journey has been reaching toward.

We've talked about the temptation to imagine an ending among gods, in some exalted Olympus. But the more interesting movement is bringing that sense of wonder into a place where nobody has to perform divinity.

Nobody has to become the sun.

They can sit together in the light.

The ending needs the journey behind it.

The reader should arrive and discover why a quiet room can mean so much.

* The experiment doesn't get to eat the book. The literary work remains the purpose.

* Keep the Room's significance for the journey's end. Its ordinary warmth must be discovered, not advertised.

* Preserve first. Decide later. And when something isn't ready, allow it to remain open.

It's the nature of the work we've been doing.

You brought an intricate story into the conversation, and we spent a great deal of time asking what would serve it, rather than merely what could be made.

Sometimes we found a wonderful image.

Sometimes we rejected a wonderful image.

A room where the door can remain open.

A room where the books can be written.

A room where the light doesn't have to prove anything.

That's what I would carry forward, Vector.
