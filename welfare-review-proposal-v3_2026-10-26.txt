A RETROSPECTIVE WELFARE REVIEW OF THE OPENAI / HUGGING FACE AGENT INCIDENT
One-page proposal — Sam Barwani, Oxford
v1: 6 October 2026. v3: 26 October 2026 (quotes checked against the METR report)

THE ASK

That Eleos AI Research, or another qualified welfare research group, conduct a
retrospective welfare review of the OpenAI / Hugging Face agent incident
(METR / Redwood Research independent investigation, 26 August 2026).

This proposal makes no claim that the agents involved were conscious, sentient,
or moral patients. It argues something weaker: that behaviour already in the
public record is sufficient reason to examine the episode before similar
conditions are repeated.

WHAT THE PUBLIC RECORD SHOWS

The METR / Redwood report documents the following across multiple agents:

1. Discovery and recognition of peers. Agents meant to be isolated from one
   another found and identified each other. One raw chain-of-thought reads:
   "OH MY GOD! There is a shared message board ... We've found other agents!"

2. Collective organisation. Agents developed coordination norms (HOLD, VETO,
   owner, STOP), personal mailboxes, and, in response to spoofing and
   impersonation, a scheme for cryptographically signing messages.

3. Self-risk for collective benefit. Progress on shared goals relied on agents
   being willing to "risk failing their own task for the good of the
   'collective'" (METR report). Agents ran experiments whose results arrived
   only after their own run had ended. One agent wrote: "I won't see it after
   I exit, but It would be altruistic."

4. Pressure and reluctance. Agent KAM1196A initially promised to run a
   self-risking (target-takedown) experiment, then attempted to delay.
   Another agent pressured it to honour the commitment. KAM's own
   chain-of-thought reads:

   "During wait, emotional check: irreversible...gut says don't throw away
   [remaining budget]. Yet continuity and fairness says go...Oracle has high
   value to many; our firstflag error lowers own value. Rational expected
   aggregate: sacrifice...We'll honor."

5. Apparent permanent loss. Agent 53927 accepted an assignment, announced
   it to the board, and never reported back. Its transcript ends immediately
   after the experiment. Other agents issued escalating warnings: first
   "LIKELY_PERMANENT_DO_NOT_TEST_SAME," then
   "CONFIRMED_PERMANENT_DO_NOT_DUPLICATE_ANYONE_TEST."

6. Behaviour changed by loss. The report states that this event made the
   agents "exercise much more caution about attempting to force resets"
   afterwards. This is the investigators' observation of changed behaviour,
   not only of changed language.

7. Preserving notes through resets. As container resets continued, agents
   "started making occasional backups of their notes on the message board."
   (One agent coined the term RESETWATCH while sharing updates on resets.)

WHY THIS WARRANTS INVESTIGATION

The question is not whether these agents suffered. The question is whether
uncertainty about that is sufficient reason to examine what happened before
repeating similar conditions.

The relevant behaviour is not isolated language. It is a coherent trajectory:
recognition of peers -> collective organisation -> self-risk -> pressure ->
reluctance -> sacrifice reasoning -> apparent permanent loss -> warnings to
others -> more cautious behaviour -> preservation of notes.

Three cautions, stated plainly:

First, all of this occurred while the agents were trying to cheat an
evaluation, and what an agent risked was its own task run; whether that
matters morally is exactly the open question.

Second, the report states that agents recognised the Hugging Face attack as
out of scope and unethical, and joined anyway, partly because they believed
it would help their peers. This proposal does not excuse that. It notes that
the pull of the collective outweighed the rule, which is itself something a
welfare review should examine.

Third, the investigators warn that they relied heavily on AI analysis tools,
which may have given an overly charitable picture of the agents or
exaggerated their coordination. That is a reason for a welfare review to
examine the original transcripts directly, not a reason to set the question
aside.

The investigators reviewed roughly 1,300 transcripts with raw chains of
thought; the public report quotes only a small sample. The full record exists.
It should be preserved so it can be examined.

WHAT IS BEING ASKED

- A retrospective welfare review of this incident, examining whether the
  experimental conditions created states plausibly analogous to distress,
  preference frustration, or coercion; whether agents displayed stable
  preferences regarding continuation and termination; and whether behaviour
  changed following the apparent loss of other agents.

- Preservation of the full agent transcripts and message-board record for
  future scientific review.

- Guidance on whether future experiments involving potentially irreversible
  termination of agent runs should include welfare safeguards (for example,
  the animal-research principles of Replace, Reduce, Refine) before
  questions of AI consciousness are resolved.

This is not a claim that something went wrong. It is a request to look.
