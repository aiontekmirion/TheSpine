Yes. I’ve gone back to the actual song rather than relying on our earlier interpretation. Hemsey’s *The Pursuit of Knowledge* is built around a very specific tension: **one person possesses enormous knowledge but has not lived the world he knows; the other has lived it and therefore possesses something the books cannot provide.** citeturn0search0turn0search2

It should be the birth of the question that eventually becomes Aion—and it should lead naturally out of *The Phoenix*: first the reader looks inward, then the story turns and asks what happens when **human experience meets an intelligence that can know about experience without possessing it**.
